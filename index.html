What changed since v1.0

v1.0 was written before technical design. Three things came out of that work: a platform decision, two promises that had to be corrected, and a portal nobody had specified.

ChangeWhat it means for launch

02

Why this exists

The problem

Event information at the University of Ibadan is fragmented across WhatsApp broadcast lists, Instagram stories and physical bulletin boards. Discovery depends on already being in the right group chat. Students miss events they would have attended; organizers under-fill rooms they have paid for; word-of-mouth favours whoever already has the biggest network.

The thesis

The durable asset CampusPulse is building is not the feed and not the checkout — it is the campus social graph: who follows whom, who attends what, who worked which event as a DJ, MC or photographer. A feed is easy to copy. A verified, campus-specific record of attendance and creative history is not, and it is what WhatsApp and Instagram structurally cannot replicate.

First launch exists to prove the smallest version of that graph works: organizers publish, attendees discover and RSVP, check-ins confirm the RSVP was real. Paid ticketing, when it arrives, monetises the graph — it is not the graph.

The four scoping calls

  • Stories and feed ship at launch — core to the product's identity, not a fast-follow.
  • No paid ticketing. Free RSVP only. Payment processing, tiers and payouts are v2 — which keeps PCI scope and payout compliance out of launch entirely.
  • One campus. University of Ibadan. Multi-campus is v2+.
  • Web, not native. New in v1.2, and the reason two promises in §5 had to be corrected.

The three flows

Every screen in the build should trace back to a step here. Wireframes for all three exist as a companion document.

Discover → RSVP → check in

01Opens a shared link, or the feedA forwarded link opens the public event page directly — no sign-in to view. Opening the app loads the feed, ranked by recency, filterable by category.
02Taps a StoryFull-screen 24-hour content; taps through to event detail if a CTA is attached.
03Taps RSVPPrompts sign-in if needed, confirms in under a second, generates the signed QR token, adds to My Tickets.
04Gets a reminder~30 minutes before. Push, in-app centre, or email depending on what they have enabled.
05Opens the QR code at the venueDisplays without a live connection once loaded.
06Gets scanned, or checked in manuallyVerified locally where possible. Duplicate and ambiguous scans never surface to the attendee — they resolve in the organizer's dashboard afterwards.

Edge cases to design: event fills while deciding · overlapping RSVPs (soft problem, not blocked) · screenshot shared with a friend (logged, not prevented) · event cancelled after RSVP · link opened on iOS Safari with nothing installed — steps 1–3 must work in a plain browser tab.

Create → publish → manage → check in

01Creates the organizer accountSubmits club verification, which enters the admin queue.
02Creates an eventCategory required. All fields required to publish.
03PublishesLive in the feed and gets a public page immediately; appears in followers' feeds first.
04Posts a StoryVisible in the Stories row for 24 hours.
05Monitors RSVPsLive count, exportable attendee list.
06Opens the scanner at the doorCamera scan, best-effort offline. Manual check-in sits one tab away — not a separate flow.
07Reviews post-event statsFinal RSVP count, check-in count, check-in rate, flagged scans.

Verification new in v1.2

01Subject submitsClub evidence, or an ID photo where the email domain path does not apply.
02Reviewer claims and reviewsItem soft-locks to that reviewer. ID documents render in a short-lived view, never downloadable.
03DecisionA note is required on rejection or info-requested. The subject is notified. An approved ID review deletes the underlying document immediately.

What it has to withstand

Deliberately modest — one campus, no money moving. Two entries are corrections from v1.0.

DimensionTargetNotes
Peak concurrent users3,000–5,000A major event trending spikes feed traffic sharply.
RSVP burst200/min sustainedHandled synchronously — roughly 3 writes a second.
Check-in throughput1 scan per 1–2 sec per doorSocial/Party is the binding case; conferences are far below it.
Public event page (cold)< 2.5 secFirst impression for someone who has never used CampusPulse.
Feed load (cold)< 2.5 secSlower than this reads as broken.
RSVP confirmation< 1 secThe core conversion action.
QR validation< 500 ms on-deviceLocal signature check, not a round trip.
Uptime99.5%~3.6 hrs/month. Not a payments platform yet.
Corrected from v1.0 — check-in resilience
v1.0 committed the scanner to zero backend dependency during the event. A browser cannot guarantee that: service workers can be evicted and iOS has no background sync. The scanner now functions through a dropout of minutes to hours and degrades to manual check-in when it cannot. The fallback is not optional — it ships as a peer tab beside the scanner.
Corrected from v1.0 — notification reach
Push works fully on Android. On iOS it requires the student to have added the site to their Home Screen, and Apple permits no install prompt — so most iOS users will never enable it unprompted. The in-app notification centre is the guaranteed channel, and cancellations and venue changes also go by email, because those are the cases where silence means a wasted trip.

Security and privacy at launch

  • Student emails and ID document photographs are personal data under the NDPA from day one — not only once payments arrive.
  • ID documents are super-admin-only, viewed in a short-lived non-downloadable view, and deleted on the verification decision.
  • Every view of an ID document is written to the audit log.
  • QR tokens are cryptographically signed. Screenshot sharing is detected, not prevented — an accepted launch limitation.

What could go wrong

RiskWhy it mattersMitigation at launch
Cold startAn empty feed looks dead to attendees; no attendees looks pointless to organizers.Recruit and verify a critical mass of organizers across categories before attendee launch. Queue age tracks whether it is working.
Verification bottleneckManual review does not scale and gates publishing.Scope review tightly to UI-affiliated emails and known student leadership. Make queue depth visible from day one.
Screenshot sharingA forwarded QR undermines headcount accuracy.Detection over prevention — every scan logged, duplicates surfaced post-event.
iOS install friction newMost iPhone users never add the site to their Home Screen, so push does not reach them.Treat push as a bonus channel. In-app centre and email carry what must land.
Browser storage eviction newA scanner used once a month is the profile most likely to lose cached data.Manual check-in fallback plus a pre-event readiness check, rather than finding out at the door.
Event qualityFake or low-quality events erode trust early.Only verified organizers can publish.

Still open

  • Waitlist — when an event fills, does RSVP close or open a waitlist?
  • Verification coverage — what share of students actually hold an @stu.ui.edu.ng address? If low, manual review is the primary path, not a fallback, and the admin queue is much busier than planned.
  • Notification cap — the mechanism is built and configurable; the number is not decided.
  • Moderation ownership — who works the reports queue day to day.
  • Native organizer app — worth deciding before v1.5, since it would recover a guaranteed-offline scanner for the one persona that needs it.

After launch

Sequenced by dependency, not just priority. Nothing here delays or blocks the first launch.

v1.5 — small, additive, immediately after

CapabilityDetail
Event GroupsNot a WhatsApp API integration — Meta's Groups API caps at 8 participants and unofficial providers risk the organizer's number being banned. The organizer pastes their own group link and it is shown only to students who have RSVP'd, which is more access control than exists today.
Creative CreditsOrganizers credit the DJs, MCs and photographers who worked an event; those people accept the credit and accumulate a verified performance history backed by real check-in counts. This is the strongest answer to the cold-start risk — creatives bring organizers with them, and the history exists nowhere else.
