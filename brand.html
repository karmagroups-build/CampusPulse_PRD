Brand story

Why CampusPulse

The name is built from two words doing two different jobs — one names the world we live in, the other names the feeling we deliver.

Campus
The audience and the context. It grounds the product in a specific, physical place — lecture halls, hostels, faculty lawns — rather than a generic ticketing app that could belong anywhere.
Pulse
The real-time energy of student life. A pulse is rhythmic, alive, and immediate — exactly what campus social activity feels like when you're plugged into it, and exactly what it feels like when you're not.
Discovery is a feed problem. Hosting is a commerce problem. Most platforms solve one and bolt the other on. CampusPulse was built as both from day one.
The model

Two products, one pulse

CampusPulse behaves differently depending on who's holding the phone — but never looks like two different apps stitched together.

For attendees
A scrollable, visual feed of what's happening — closer to Instagram Stories than a listings page. Tap an event, RSVP or buy a ticket, show up with a QR code. Discovery should feel like browsing, not searching.
For organizers
Real ticketing infrastructure — tiers, pricing, capacity, payouts, check-in scanning, attendee analytics. Closer to Eventbrite's back office than a Google Form. Hosting a paid event should feel professional, not improvised.
Logo

Mark & wordmark

The mark reads as a "C" for CampusPulse, but its open form and the line cutting through it are deliberate: unity on the outside, a live pulse on the inside.

01

The C-form

An open, unbroken arc represents community and unity — a circle intentionally left incomplete so it always looks like it's mid-motion, never static.

02

The pulse line

A single continuous stroke cuts through the form like a heartbeat monitor — the same visual shorthand as a live signal, standing in for real-time campus activity.

03

Continuous stroke, no lift

Both elements are drawn as one uninterrupted path. The mark should never be redrawn with breaks, gaps, or separated pieces — the continuity is the point.

MINIMUM CLEAR SPACE = HEIGHT OF THE MARK ON ALL SIDES · MINIMUM DIGITAL SIZE = 24PX

Logo · misuse

Protect the mark

The mark is drawn as a single gradient-filled or single-color path. It should never be redrawn, recolored piecemeal, or broken apart.

Don't stretch or distort the proportions
Don't rotate the mark off its fixed axis
Don't outline it or break the continuous stroke
Don't place on low-contrast backgrounds

Voice

Two registers, one voice

CampusPulse never sounds like campus administration or like a payments processor. It shifts register by context — casual and current for attendees, precise and trustworthy for organizers handling money — without ever sounding like two different brands.

01

Stories surface urgency

Organizers post short-lived updates — almost-sold-out warnings, lineup drops, last-call reminders — the same disposable, glanceable format students already check daily.

02

The feed is the home screen

No tab called "Browse" or "Search." Opening the app drops you straight into what's happening, ranked by time and proximity — the way a social feed opens on activity, not a menu.

03

RSVP and buy live in one tap

Free events get an RSVP button. Paid events get the same button, doing more — no separate "checkout" experience that breaks the scroll.

Organizer experience

Real infrastructure, not a Google Form

Anyone can post an event to a group chat. CampusPulse is for organizers who need to actually sell tickets, track capacity, and get paid — with the trust signals that come from real payment infrastructure.

01

Payouts are always visible

Organizers see exactly what they'll receive, and when — the platform fee is never a surprise buried in a settlement email.

02

Check-in is the trust moment

QR scanning at the door is where "an app" becomes "the way this event actually runs." The scanner UI has to feel instant and unambiguous — valid or not, nothing in between.

03

Tiers, not just tickets

Regular and VIP pricing, capacity limits, and sales-close timers give organizers the same controls a professional venue would expect, scaled down to a hall committee's budget.
